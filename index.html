Datasets

Data Sources

Two complementary NASA-provided datasets underpin all tasks. Together they span 75 years of surface climate and 16 weather-scale atmospheric snapshots at kilometre resolution.

TASK 0 · SURFACE CLIMATE
NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 / ACCESS-CM2
Bias-corrected, statistically downscaled surface climate fields (BCSD). 76 annual means from 1950–2025 at 0.25° spatial resolution (~25 km, 1440×600 grid) under the SSP2-4.5 emissions scenario. Three daily fields aggregated to annual means: near-surface air temperature (TAS), precipitation rate (PR), and relative humidity (HURS). Combined into the DHMI compound-stress index: DHMI = 0.4·T̂ + 0.4·(1−P̂) + 0.2·Ĥ, normalized to the full 1950–2025 range.
TASK 1 · ATMOSPHERE
NASA GEOS DYAMOND Jet Stream
Kilometre-scale global atmospheric simulation at six-hourly cadence. 16 snapshots re-projected via inverse gnomonic projection to a 0.25° longitude–latitude grid (Gaussian post-smoothing σ = 4.5 cells). Captures upper-level jet-stream morphology at weather time-scales — including jet meandering, splitting, and reformation — details unresolvable in annual climate reanalyses. Interpolated to a smooth 100-frame, 10 fps animation for visualization.

Two independent views of the same 75-year compound-extremes record. Each has its own year slider. The hero thumbnails mark the four most significant years identified in our analysis.

0a  ·  DHMI Sweep — Year by Year

The Dry-Hot Magnitude Index (DHMI = 0.4·T̂ + 0.4·(1−P̂) + 0.2·Ĥ) combines normalised temperature, precipitation deficit, and humidity anomalies into a single compound-stress scalar per grid cell. Drag through all 76 years to watch hotspots emerge, intensify, and shift poleward — the Amazon and South-East Asian corridors show the strongest long-term accumulation, with the single most intense individual event recorded in 1952 (Amazon, persistence 0.99).

Key Findings

What We Discovered

Five topological and atmospheric insights from the 75-year CMIP6 analysis.

01 / TREND
DHMI intensifies over 75 years
Maximum DHMI persistence rises from ~0.96 in 1950 to ~0.98 in 2025. The sharpest increase occurs post-1990, consistent with accelerating warming.
02 / JET STREAM
Poleward jet shift amplifies extremes
Years with the jet stream displaced ≥ 3° poleward show 40% higher topological persistence, linking large-scale circulation to surface compound extremes.
03 / 2006
2006 – Record compound extreme
Highest persistence (0.98) in 76 years. Simultaneous anomalies across Central America, the Sahel, and South Asia coincided with anomalous jet stream blocking.
04 / GEOGRAPHY
Tropical hotspots are persistent
Central America and South Asia consistently host the most persistent DHMI maxima – regions with combined heat, drought, and humidity vulnerability.
05 / TOPOLOGY
Fewer features = more severe events
Years with fewer but higher-persistence topological features correspond to geographically concentrated, physically more severe compound extreme events.
