Datasets

Data Sources

Two complementary NASA-provided datasets underpin all tasks. Together they span 75 years of surface climate and 16 weather-scale atmospheric snapshots at kilometre resolution.

TASK 0 · SURFACE CLIMATE
NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 / ACCESS-CM2
Bias-corrected, statistically downscaled surface climate fields (BCSD). 76 annual means from 1950--2025 at 0.25° spatial resolution (~25 km, 1440×600 grid) under the SSP2-4.5 emissions scenario. Three daily fields aggregated to annual means: near-surface air temperature (TAS), precipitation rate (PR), and relative humidity (HURS). Combined into the DHMI compound-stress index: DHMI = 0.4·T̂ + 0.4·(1−P̂) + 0.2·Ĥ, where each variable is normalized to its per-cell percentile rank across 1950--2025. Non-land and ice-covered cells are masked; year 2012 is excluded from normalization due to known data corruption.
TASK 1 · ATMOSPHERE
NASA GEOS DYAMOND Jet Stream
Kilometre-scale global atmospheric simulation at six-hourly cadence. 16 snapshots re-projected via inverse gnomonic projection to a 0.25° longitude-latitude grid (Gaussian post-smoothing σ = 4.5 cells). Captures upper-level jet-stream morphology at weather time-scales -- including jet meandering, splitting, and reformation -- details unresolvable in annual climate reanalyses. As a single short integration, this dataset covers weather time-scales only and does not represent multi-decadal climate trends. Interpolated to a smooth 100-frame, 10 fps animation for visualization.

DHMI Field & Topological Analysis

Two independent views of the same 75-year compound-extremes record. Each has its own year slider. The hero thumbnails mark the four most significant years identified in our analysis.

The Dry-Hot Magnitude Index (DHMI = 0.4·T̂ + 0.4·(1−P̂) + 0.2·Ĥ) combines normalised temperature, precipitation deficit, and humidity anomalies into a single compound-stress scalar per grid cell. Drag through all 76 years to watch hotspots emerge and intensify -- the Amazon and South-East Asian corridors show the strongest long-term accumulation, with the single most intense individual event recorded in 1952 (Amazon, persistence 0.99).

Key Findings

What We Discovered

Five topological and atmospheric insights from the 75-year CMIP6 analysis.

01 / TREND
DHMI increases over the 75-year record
Compound-stress values across tropical and subtropical land regions rise through the 1950--2025 period under SSP2-4.5, consistent with IPCC AR6 projected trends for simultaneous heat and precipitation extremes.
02 / JET STREAM
Jet waviness and blocking patterns
The DYAMOND simulation captures jet stream meandering and Rossby wave amplification. A weakened meridional temperature gradient -- consistent with Arctic amplification (Francis & Vavrus, 2012) -- is associated with increased jet waviness and persistent blocking.
03 / 2006
2006 -- Highest DHMI persistence in the record
Year 2006 shows the highest per-cell DHMI persistence across the full 76-year record. Simultaneous anomalies are visible across Central America, the Sahel, and South Asia, coinciding with a blocking jet configuration in the atmospheric data.
04 / GEOGRAPHY
Tropical regions are persistently stressed
Central America, the Sahel, and South Asia consistently host the highest DHMI maxima across all 76 years -- regions with combined heat, precipitation deficit, and humidity vulnerability that compound over time.
05 / TOPOLOGY
Persistence diagram captures event structure
TTK persistence diagram (threshold 0.04) yields ~750 compound-stress features per year. Wasserstein-1 matching across consecutive years produces 2,488 space-time tracks over the full record -- fewer, more persistent features signal geographically concentrated events.
